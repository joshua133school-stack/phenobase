4. Quality control

New records enter as unverified. The national node checks each season's records and sets verified or flagged. Automatic checks:

  1. Order: stages of one site and season must be in the order of their codes (for example 60 before 65 before 69).
  2. Range: the date must be within 3 standard deviations of the mean for that crop, stage and region, or carry a note explaining why.
  3. Interval: intervals that are biologically impossible (for example full flowering to harvest maturity of apple in under 60 days) are flagged.
  4. Duplicates: one record per site, crop, cultivar, stage and season.
  5. Location: coordinates must fall inside the stated country and province.

Flagged records are not deleted. They stay in the database with their flag so the decision can be reviewed.

5. Observer privacy and data ownership

  • Farmers own their observations. They choose to share them with phenorelay and can withdraw their records at any time through their national node.
  • Observer names, phone numbers and exact farm addresses are held only by the national node and are never published. Published records carry a pseudonymous observer ID.
  • Published coordinates are rounded to about 1 km.
  • Each participating farmer receives their own records back every season, with the regional average for comparison.
  • The project follows the FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable) for data and the CARE principles (collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, ethics) for the communities who provide it.

6. Licence and citation

Proposed licence: observation data CC BY 4.0 (reuse permitted with attribution); guideline texts CC BY 4.0; website source code under an open-source licence to be confirmed by the project board.

Cite the database as:

phenorelay: crop phenology observation network for Asia (year).
Release <version>. https://github.com/joshua133school-stack/phenobase. Accessed <date>.

Cite single records by their record ID; each record page shows a citation line.
