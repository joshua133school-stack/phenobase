1. Why this project

Phenology is the timing of recurring events in the life of a plant: bud burst, flowering, fruit ripening, leaf fall. These dates move with the weather and, over decades, with the climate. For a farmer they decide frost risk at flowering, when labour is needed, when fruit can be sold and which cultivars still suit the region.

Asia produces much of the world's rice, fruit and vegetables, yet very little Asian crop phenology reaches the international databases used for climate and agricultural research. The farmers who see these stages every day are rarely asked to record them in a standard form. PhenoBase gives them that form, in their own language, and keeps the records in one open, comparable database.

3. Aims

  1. Build a long-term, open record of fruit and field crop phenology across Asia, observed by farmers.
  2. Publish a simple, illustrated observation guideline in the main languages of the region.
  3. Use international BBCH stage codes so that Asian records can be combined with European and American data.
  4. Return value to farmers: each farmer receives their own record each season, compared with their region.
  5. Provide data for frost-risk warnings, harvest planning, cultivar choice and climate-change research.

4. Scope

Crops40 crops in the first list: temperate fruit (apple, Asian pear, peach, ume, grape, persimmon, jujube…), subtropical and tropical fruit (citrus, lychee, longan, mango, durian…), cereals (rice, wheat, barley, maize, millet), legumes and oilseeds, vegetables and root crops.
Countries18 target countries: Korea, Japan, China, Taiwan, Mongolia, Viet Nam, Thailand, Lao PDR, Cambodia, Myanmar, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, India, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka. The pilot starts in Korea.
ObserversFarmers first, with support from extension services, farmer cooperatives, agricultural schools and research institutes.
StagesA small set of core stages per crop type that farmers can recognise without instruments.

7. How to join

Farmers and farmer groupsRead the guideline, choose your trees or plot, and start recording with the recording sheet or the online form.
Extension services, cooperativesOrganise a local farmer group and act as group leader. We provide training material.
Universities and research institutesBecome a national node: coordinate observers, review translations, check data quality.
TranslatorsTranslate the guideline into your language. See translation status.
DevelopersThe website is open source. See the repository.

8. Contact

Until national nodes are established, please contact the project through the issue tracker. Use the labels join, translation, data or website.
