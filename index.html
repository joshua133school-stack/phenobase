PhenoBase: Asian Farmer Phenology Database

PhenoBase is a database of when crops reach their life-cycle stages, such as bud burst, flowering, ripening, heading and harvest. Farmers record these stages in their own orchards and fields across East, Southeast and South Asia. It covers fruit crops (apple, pear, persimmon, citrus, mango, lychee, durian and others) and field crops (rice, wheat, soybean, vegetables and root crops).

Stages are recorded with the international BBCH codes, so records from Korea, Japan, Viet Nam, India and other countries can be compared with each other and with European and North American phenology networks.

Why this database

  • International phenology databases hold little data from Asia, and very little from Korea.
  • National phenology programmes in the region mainly track wild species, animals and insects. Cultivated fruit and field crops are mostly missing.
  • Farmers already notice when their trees bud, flower and ripen. Writing these dates down in a standard way builds a long-term record that is useful to the farmer, to extension services and to climate research.
