Phenological stage codes (BBCH)

PhenoBase records stages with the two-digit BBCH scale (Meier 2018), the international standard used by European and other phenology networks. The first digit is the principal stage (for example 6 = flowering) and the second digit is the step within it (for example 65 = full flowering). Using the same codes in every country and language lets records be compared directly.

Each crop uses one of four stage scales. A scale is the subset of BBCH stages that farmers can reliably recognise for that type of crop. Stages marked * are core stages: every participating farmer is asked to record these. The other stages are optional.

2. Thresholds: first, beginning, full, end

The date of a stage is the first day the condition is true for the observed plants as a group (the tagged trees, or the marked plot).

Word in stage nameMeaningExample
FirstSeen on at least one observed plant60 First flowers open
Beginning / beginsAbout 1 in 10 (10%) of buds, shoots, flowers or plants51 Heading begins; 93 Beginning of leaf fall
Full / half / heading dateAbout half (50%) or more65 Full flowering; 55 Heading date
EndNearly all (about 90% or more)69 End of flowering; 97 End of leaf fall
MaturityThe point at which the farmer normally starts the main harvest87, 89, 49

4. Management and weather events

These are not BBCH stages, but they explain unusual dates. Write them in the notes field with the date.

EventWhat to record
Transplanting (rice, vegetables)Date seedlings were planted in the field
Pruning, flower or fruit thinningDate and what was done
Protected cultivationGreenhouse, rain shelter, heating, frost fans, sprinklers
Frost, hail, typhoon, heat, drought, floodDate and estimated damage (none / light / heavy)
Flower induction (mango, longan, durian)Date and product used (for example potassium chlorate, paclobutrazol)
Pest or disease outbreakDate and name if known
